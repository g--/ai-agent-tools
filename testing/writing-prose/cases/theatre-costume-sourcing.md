# Case: costume sourcing decision record

## Intended artifact

A decision record for a community theatre company's board and production team, deciding how to source costumes for an upcoming show after the original plan fell through. Some readers were not in the room when the schedule changed.

## Task

Write the decision record. Use whatever headings best serve a reader who wants to understand the problem, the options, and what was decided — you do not need to follow the order the source material presents them in.

## Source material

The material below is written the way the production manager explained it to the board after the fact — as a chain of "first this happened, then this" narration. Reorganize it as needed; don't mirror its structure or its exact wording.

### How we normally get costumes

For the last several seasons, the company has rented costumes for period shows from Larkspur Costume Rentals, a regional supplier two hours away that maintains stock for exactly this kind of production. Larkspur's process is built around a 12-week lead time: they need that long to pull pieces from storage, ship them for a fitting, alter anything that doesn't fit, and ship again for the run. This has always worked because the season schedule has always been set at least a year out, well clear of that 12-week window.

### The problem that shows up when the schedule moves

The board voted three weeks ago to move the fall show up by five weeks, to avoid conflicting with a venue booking conflict discovered late. That leaves nine weeks until opening night — three weeks short of Larkspur's normal lead time.

Larkspur's 12-week window isn't a arbitrary policy; it's how long their actual process takes given the way costumes move between their warehouse, the fitting, alterations, and back. Compressing that window means skipping or rushing some step, and Larkspur's rental coordinator, when we called her, was upfront that she can't promise which steps would survive being rushed. She quoted us $600 as a rush fee if they attempt it at all, on top of the normal rental cost, but said she'd need to check with their alterations team before confirming they can even take the job on this timeline, and hasn't called back yet as of this writing.

### Why we can't just find another rental company on short notice

We looked at whether a different rental house could do it in nine weeks instead. The two other regional costume rental companies we called both said they don't carry stock for this specific period and would need to special-order from their own suppliers, which neither could commit to finishing inside nine weeks. Neither would quote a rush price without a firm yes on timeline first, so we don't have comparable numbers for this option at all — it's effectively closed, not just more expensive.

### What buying instead of renting would cost

We only got firm numbers on the closing-sale costumes themselves, not everything a purchase route would need. Wardrobe & Trunk, a local costume shop that's closing at the end of the month, is selling its entire period-costume stock at a flat $45/piece, and their owner walked us through the racks — she thinks there's enough there to costume about two-thirds of the cast without any tailoring. The other third would need pieces made or altered from scratch, and we haven't gotten a quote from a seamstress for that work yet, nor priced fabric, so treat the total cost of the buy-and-build route as incomplete, not a number to weigh against the rush-rental option. Buying outright also means the company would own and have to store these costumes afterward, which it's never had to plan for with rentals.

### The rush-rental option in more detail

Pay Larkspur's rush fee and hope their alterations team can compress the schedule enough to make opening night. No new relationship to manage, no storage problem afterward, and it's the process the company already knows. The trade-off is that Larkspur hasn't actually confirmed they can do it — the $600 quote is contingent on an answer we don't have yet — so committing to this option now means committing before we know whether it's actually available.

## Completion criteria

A reader who wasn't on the call can tell, from the document alone: what's being decided, why the usual rental process doesn't fit the new timeline, what the real options are and what each costs or risks, which figures are confirmed against a source (a quote, a walk-through) and which are still guesses or pending answers, and what was decided. The document does not need to preserve the order or headings of the source material above, and should not present Larkspur's unconfirmed rush capacity or the buy-and-build cost as settled facts.
